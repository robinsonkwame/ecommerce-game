Multichannel businesses operate their various channels independently, leading to a disjointed customer experience and internal inefficiencies. 

In contrast, omnichannel businesses integrate all their channels to provide a seamless, consistent experience for customers and more efficient operations for the company.

The transition from multichannel to omnichannel is characterized by technological, organizational, and market changes, affecting technologies, organizations, and markets simultaneously.


Some of the issues with e-commerce include data security and theft, ethical e-commerce practices, competitor analysis, and customer loyalty. 
Data Security and Theft: IBM estimates that the average cost of a data breach on an organization is $3.9 million dollars and $150 for every record that has been lost or compromised. 
Ethical Issues: Issues include making sure product listing are accurate, maintaining accordance with online selling laws, and maintaining web accessibility for persons with disabilities.

Customer Loyalty: it is estimated that it can cost up to 5 times more to acquire a new customer than it costs to retain one. 
the success rate of selling to an existing customer is 60-70 percent versus only 5-15 percent for a new customer


Paper analyzing 119 e-commerce users (in Malaysia), found the following positive and negative factors for customer loyalty:

Word of Mouth was the strongest factor
Then, trustworthiness (measured by evaluating company perceived integrity, ability, and benevolence)

Following that were: switching cost (“I’ll lose these points if I leave!”), and web design. Security was not found to be a significant factor.
Therefore, it can be helpful to focus on:
Increasing Word of Mouth while engaging in customer service that builds up or maintains perceived trustworthiness
Look into lock-in strategies to make it harder for customers to leave: points, membership loyalty plans, etc. etc.

Disruption phases: High scores for innovation and influencers, but with increased risk. Customer service becomes more important but still not dominant.

Consolidation: Customer service becomes more valuable as companies try to retain customers. Innovation remains important but becomes more expensive. Influencer impact starts to decrease.

Winner Takes ALL: Customer service becomes crucial for retaining market share. Innovation is still important for staying ahead. Influencer marketing becomes less effective as the market consolidates around a few major players.

These allocations reflect the changing landscape where early disruption favors bold moves and innovation, while later stages reward customer retention and incremental improvements to maintain market dominance.


What I was trying to simulate:

Disruption Phase:
Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC): Lower for innovative strategies
Growth Rate: Higher for disruptive businesses
Market Share: Rapidly changing
Innovation Score: Higher rewards for novel approaches

Consolidation Phase:
Market Concentration: Fewer players control larger market share
Customer Retention Rate: Becomes more important
Barrier to Entry: Increases for new players

Winner-Takes-All Phase:
Network Effects: Exponential rewards for market leaders
Market Dominance: Very high market share for top player(s)
Profit Margins: Higher for dominant players
Data Advantage: Leaders gain more insights from larger user bases
Ecosystem Lock-in: Penalties for users trying to switch platforms
